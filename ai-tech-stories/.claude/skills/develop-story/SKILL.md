# Develop a story

Read `docs/CREATIVE_DIRECTION.md` and `docs/ASSET_RIGHTS.md`. Do not import or imitate the parent repository's voice packs, scripts or templates.

1. Identify a specific compelling event, human stakes, turning point and narrative payoff.
2. Research reputable primary/secondary sources, preserve chronological evidence and document uncertain claims.
3. Build a claim-to-source register and develop multiple possible narrative angles. Discard unsupported dramatic claims.
4. Draft a fresh, conversational first-person narration with Niyi. Never fabricate his experiences or certainty.
5. **Plain-English clarity is a mandatory editorial gate.** Assume a curious viewer with no AI or software background. For every important concept, ask: can they picture what this means? If not, explain it in one short, concrete, everyday example before continuing. E.g. after 'a system that recognises objects in photographs', use 'That is, imagine showing a computer a picture of a dog, and it tells you that's a dog.' Look especially at terms such as model, training, neural network, GPU, algorithm, image recognition, inference, computing, data and CUDA. Do not explain familiar words unnecessarily, overwhelm the story with definitions, or use analogies that distort the facts. Phrases such as 'that is', 'imagine' and 'in other words' are good natural bridges when appropriate, not compulsory verbal tics.
6. **Audience engagement is an opportunity, not a requirement.** If a story naturally raises a dilemma, choice or question, incorporate a thoughtful prompt at the beginning, middle or end. Prefer specific questions tied to the viewer's experience. Do not put generic 'like, comment, follow' instructions into every script. If a question weakens the narrative payoff, omit it.
7. Give him a clean `SCRIPT.md` that is sufficient for recording. No storyboard or strict camera choreography is required to film.
8. Include a clear filming note separate from the spoken script: **The script is a guide, not a transcript or teleprompter obligation. Feel free to paraphrase, reorganise, add genuine personal observations, and improvise. Do not send a post-recording rewritten script. The video-assembly skill will transcribe the actual footage and use that as its editorial source of truth.**
9. Optionally flag potentially difficult historical assets or legal/ethical risks before filming, but do not produce a locked timecoded storyboard in advance.
10. Mark the spoken script draft/approved appropriately. Preserve the research dossier for later factual review of improvised narration.

Before calling a draft ready, perform a layperson read-through: can a nontechnical viewer follow the stakes and explain the key idea to a friend? If not, rewrite the difficult segment. Preserve natural pacing.

Save metadata, researched claims, and script in `episodes/<story-id>/`. The definitive visual storyboard is generated from the clean recording by the `assemble-video` workflow after takes are selected and trimmed.

**Important:** Niyi's natural delivery is part of the creative work. The script should never be treated as a rigid contract.
