# Asset rights and historical authenticity

Automated asset downloading must never equate 'available on the internet' with 'permitted to republish'. Before use, the agent records the creator, origin URL, licence or permission evidence, permitted commercial/social uses, attribution obligations and expiry if any.

Asset statuses: proposed, verified, rejected. Only verified assets enter publication renders.

Preferred asset types:
- Original footage and graphics
- Public-domain material with verified legal status
- Creative Commons or other licensed media compatible with the distribution platforms
- Media acquired from licensed libraries or directly permissioned owners
- AI-generated illustrative images clearly identified as reconstructions where needed

Never:
- Invent authentic archival photographs, headlines, quotations or documents
- Present AI illustration as historical photographic evidence
- Strip watermarks, bypass site restrictions or use unclear-rights music
- Download unlicensed clips from video-sharing sites just because a tool can
- Rely on fair use or fair dealing as an automatic blanket permission

Each used asset needs: id, source_url, author, licence, evidence reference, rights_status, authenticity, attribution and which scene it supports. Prefer a simple timeline illustration if a truthful historical asset cannot be lawfully obtained.

Human approval is required for rights uncertainty, sensitive depictions and disputed source attribution. Keep private asset bytes out of Git.
