Topic: AI and Office Jobs Timeline (1–18 Months)

Microsoft CEO recently said we have just one year to 18 months for AI to take over most office jobs. That means most computer-based work could be automated within a year and a half. We're talking lawyers, accountants, project managers, software developers, name it. And I think this is a very serious timeline. His logic is simple. AI is already performing near-human level on many professional tasks. For example, Microsoft engineers, actually almost all engineers, are reportedly using AI for most of their coding work. Of course, it comes with a lot of nuances, but hey, the thing is, most tech jobs are pattern-based. So sooner or later, AI will figure things out for sure. But here is where it gets tricky. Automating some tasks is not entirely the same as replacing jobs. Real-life work has some nuances, you know, accountability, internal politics, risk, and some level of ambiguity. Things might be different when I wake up tomorrow, but the consumer AI we use right now still struggles with a lot of real-life instances. But one thing is clear, the pressure is real. Here is a real takeaway. The company doesn't need AI to be 100% for this timeline. If AI handles 60 to 70% of the routine desk work, restructuring can already begin. And this exact same thing happened at my workplace a few years ago. So do this. Look at what you do every day. Which ones require real human insight? And which ones are routine, that is, they have a set pattern. Start paying a lot of attention to that first category and see how much AI you can leverage for the second category.
